# Position

Step 4 of the review: what the evidence allows this repository to claim, and what it would add. Each "not found" is a statement about these searches, not about the world. Terms are in [frame](frame.md).

## Known

- A process from problem to evaluated result exists in research: six activities, a nominal order, any entry point (Peffers 2006).
- Practice has its own frameworks for the same span: CRISP-DM, Scrum, Shape Up (Chapman 2000; Schwaber 2020; Singer 2019).
- Avoidable rework is large by expert report, and hasty specification is a named source (Boehm 2001).
- Practice guides for a project's folder and log agree (Noble 2009; Wilson 2017; Sandve 2013).
- A call for a review before new work exists in medicine (Lund 2016), with the neglect measured (Glasziou 2014, secondary).

## Not found

- A study that isolates a written scope, a named baseline or a list of the person's decisions and measures rework or round trips.
- A study that a set of files lets a second person resume.
- A study of a plan written as a structured text, beyond outlines for writing.
- A review of design science process models, and a review of what a project trail holds.
- A literature-review step in the DSR process of Peffers 2006. Hevner 2004 was not opened, so this holds for that paper only.

## What the repository can claim

- Under **convention, untested**: scope and objectives first, a named baseline as the exit of a review, a four-file trail.
- Under **supported with caveat**: hasty specification is a major source of avoidable rework (Boehm 2001).
- It cannot claim a measured cut in round trips.
- It cannot claim a fixed cost ratio for late defects.

## What this repository adds

- The five steps joined: frame, review, state of the art, position, baseline, each a topic, finished by a test the field's models leave implicit.
- A baseline step that the DSR process places inside objectives and evaluation.
- A defined trail, with a boundary to the document genres.
- A way to test it: [baseline](baseline.md) sets out a comparison, not run.

## Where practice runs ahead of the evidence

- Scrum, Shape Up and CRISP-DM prescribe goals and success criteria before work; the parts read cite no measurement.
- The trail guides prescribe files; no study tests them.

## Where evidence is ignored by practice

- The delayed-issue figure of 100 times is repeated in practice while a 171-project analysis found no such effect (Menzies 2017, secondary).
