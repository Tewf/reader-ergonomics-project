# Name the baseline

**Claim:** a review is finished when it names the baseline the result will be compared with, and says how.

- **Grade:** convention, untested for the effect; the neglect of prior evidence is measured, descriptive.
- **Evidence:** [review, strand B](../../evidence/project-method/review.md); [baseline](../../evidence/project-method/baseline.md); Lund 2016; Glasziou 2014 (secondary); Peffers 2006, activities 2 and 5; Chapman 2000, phase 1.
- **Check:** `project-shape` (error, warning): the baseline part has a `Named baseline:` line that is not empty or a placeholder; a missing `Compared by:` line is a warning. That the baseline is the right one, or that the comparison was run, is judgment.
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- End the review with a part that names one baseline: the method, result or process the work is set against.
- Add a line saying how the comparison is made: the unit, what is counted, and the grade a small result earns.
- Say when the comparison is named and not run.

## Why

- Evidence-based research calls for a systematic review before new work (Lund 2016).
- New trials rarely set their findings in the context of such a review (Glasziou 2014, abstract).
- The design science process has a comparison target in its objectives and evaluation, but no step for a review (Peffers 2006).
- CRISP-DM names success criteria in its first phase, the nearest practice (Chapman 2000).

## Limits

- No source found measures that naming a baseline changes the outcome of a project.
- A baseline named in a placeholder phrase passes the check; a reader judges it.
