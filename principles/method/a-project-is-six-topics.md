# A project is six topics

**Claim:** a project is a folder of six parts, problem, scope, review, baseline, plan and trail, each written as a topic.

- **Grade:** convention, untested; an outline raised judged writing quality in an experiment (Kellogg 1988, secondary), and a plan written as a topic is not tested.
- **Evidence:** [position](../../evidence/project-method/position.md); [review, answers to the four questions](../../evidence/project-method/review.md); [decision 0003](../../decisions/0003-a-project-is-six-topics.md); Peffers 2006; Kellogg 1988 (secondary).
- **Check:** `project-shape` (errors, warnings): each project root that declares the shape has its six parts, each part announces the topic shape and holds its own required rows or headings.
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- Declare the [project shape](../../shapes/project.md) on a line labelled `Shape:` in the root.
- Write each part as a topic: a title, a summary, then the body, with the topic shape announced.
- Keep to the six parts; a job that needs fewer is not a project in this sense.

## Why

- The parts follow the activities of the design science process from problem to evaluation (Peffers 2006), with the review and the baseline made steps of their own.
- A part with its own rows or headings can be checked by a program, so a missing part is an error and not a matter of taste.

## Limits

- Six parts is a convention, and may cost a small job more than it saves (decision 0003).
- The shape ends where the genres begin: a document the project produces keeps the shape of its genre.
